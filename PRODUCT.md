## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, weighted equally:

- **Back office.** Brightloop's admin (sets up products, plans, taxes, discounts and staff), internal staff (handle customers and subscriptions day to day with limited access), and the finance lead (reviews active subscriptions, revenue, payments and overdue invoices at month end). They are at a desk working through lists, such as hundreds of subscriptions on billing day, and need to scan, act and move on.
- **Customers.** Businesses from small shops on monthly plans to large companies on yearly contracts. They sign in to a portal to accept quotations, manage their own subscriptions within the plan's rules, and view, print or pay off their invoices without emailing anyone.

## Product Purpose

LoopBill is the single system that runs a subscription business end to end: products and plans, quotation templates, subscriptions, automatic recurring invoicing with taxes and discounts, payment recording, and revenue reporting. It replaces spreadsheets, hand-typed invoices and manual bank-statement matching. Success means renewals are never missed, invoices are never wrong, billed twice or skipped, and anyone can say at any moment how much recurring revenue the company has and what is overdue.

## Positioning

Billing correctness is the product. Periods are anchored to the start date, billing runs are idempotent and safe under concurrency, every missed period is caught up rather than skipped, discount usage limits are exact, and money is integer-exact. The interface should make that trust visible: clear lifecycle states, an activity trail on every record, and figures that always reconcile.

## Operating Context

- Subscription lifecycle: Draft → Quotation → Confirmed → Active (or Paused) → Closed. Invoice lifecycle: Draft → Confirmed → Paid (or Cancelled).
- Billing day: a billing run creates draft invoices in bulk, and staff confirm and send them in batches.
- Payments arrive by bank transfer, UPI, card, cheque or cash and are recorded against invoices, including part payments.
- Quotations and invoices are emailed to customers and printed or saved as PDF.
- Month-end review: the finance lead filters the overview by date range and plan.
- Scale: thousands of subscriptions; every page must respond in under two seconds.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Roles: Admin (full control, the only role that can add staff or change catalog, taxes and discounts), Staff (day-to-day work, catalog read-only), Customer (portal; sees only their own records).
- Accounts: one account per email. Passwords must be more than 8 characters with an uppercase letter, a lowercase letter and a special character. Password reset works by an emailed single-use link.
- Plans bill daily, weekly, monthly or yearly, with a minimum quantity, availability dates, and per-plan rules for auto-close, early close, pause and renew.
- Product variants add an extra price (for example, a brand variant adds ₹560).
- Stack: Next.js 16 (App Router, Server Actions), PostgreSQL 17 with Prisma 7, Tailwind CSS 4.
- Not built yet: online payment collection (payments are recorded by staff), and customers switching add-ons on or off themselves.

## Brand Commitments

- Product name **LoopBill** and its ring-and-dot logo mark.
- **Brightloop Software** is the company using LoopBill; its name appears on invoices, quotations and emails.
- India locale: amounts in INR (₹) with Indian digit grouping, and GST-style tax rates.
- The deep green accent (#1f6f5c) is the brand color.

## Evidence on Hand

- Demo data generated by the real billing engine (`prisma/seed.ts`); a bulk mode loads about 4,000 subscriptions and 20,000 invoices.
- Measured performance: under 0.2s per page on the production server with the bulk dataset.
- There are no real customers, testimonials or case studies. Do not invent any.

## Product Principles

1. Correctness over cleverness: never show a number that doesn't reconcile.
2. Status at a glance: every subscription and invoice says where it stands and what can happen next.
3. Built for volume: favour lists, filters, bulk actions and keyboard flow for people processing hundreds of records.
4. Self-service within the rules: customers can do whatever their plan permits without contacting staff.
5. Every change leaves a trail: who did what, and when, on every record.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: text and UI contrast, full keyboard operation, visible focus, and screen-reader labels. Colour is never the only way status is shown.
